Extend Place Value to Decimals

The same digit can be worth very different amounts. It all depends on its place, even after the decimal point.

Today I can…
  • tell the value of a digit in the tenths, hundredths and thousandths places
  • explain that a digit is worth 10 times as much one place to the left, and 110 as much one place to the right

Reflect

Think about it

How is the place value pattern in decimals like the pattern in whole numbers?

See an example answer

It is the same pattern on both sides of the decimal point. Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right, and 110 of the place to its left. For example, 300 is 10 times 30, and 0.3 is 10 times 0.03.
